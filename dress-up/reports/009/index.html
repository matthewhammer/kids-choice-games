One size, every view

How big is the friend in each view of a place, against the doors they go through, and how do we keep one friend the same size inside and out when the player shapes the friend? The game at 0.0.303: 26 script parts, 22,743 lines, 139 checks. Measured in the game itself, with the default friend, an adult (size 150), a small child (size 50) and a short-legged friend.

Short answers.
  1. Inside, the friend's size follows their own body; outside on the tree's porches and bridges, it did not. The bridges set everyone to one height, smaller than inside, and a tree room's door to the next house was too low even for the default friend: so the default crawled through it inside, yet walked in through the same doorway from the porch, small (Matthew's save, legs 140, is the default). Since 0.0.302 both views scale the body the same way, and the door fits the default.
  2. One reference body, doors made for it, one rule. The default friend takes 0.68 to 0.91 of every door made for it, inside and out; any friend crawls where their crown would be over 0.97 of the door. So the default and a child walk, an adult crawls, the same both ways.
  3. Three doors were too low for the default and were raised: a tree room's door to the next house (1.27 of it), the witch's door out (1.10), the greenhouse's arches (1.04).
  4. Left for a decision: the house's front door is five times the friend's height, because the house is drawn without the perspective the friend gets there; and the pictures entered whole (the witch's hut, the tents, the cave) have no door to judge from outside.
  5. Why it is hard here: games size doors to one known character; here the player shapes the body, so every door is judged at run time against the body as it is, and every view must scale that body, never replace it with a fixed height.

Why a friend the player shapes makes this harder

  • A game's character is a known sprite. Doors, ledges and bridges are drawn for one known height; a different character means new art, checked by eye.
  • Here the body is the player's. Legs from short to very long, a head, arms and a size slider: the height is a setting, so every door has to be judged at run time, against the body as it is now.
  • And each view has its own camera. The rooms draw in perspective, the tree outside as a flat picture with its own scale, a far view smaller still: a rule like 'the friend is 60 tall here' silently drops the body's shape, as the bridges did.
  • So the doors are sized to a reference, and the rule decides. One reference body (the default), doors made for it (it takes 0.68 to 0.91 of each), and one rule for every door, inside and out (crawl over 0.97); every other body follows from its own height.

What next

  1. 1. Decide the house scene's scale: the house drawn at the friend's scale where its door stands (smaller on the screen), or left as a picture; either way, a check in the series of tools/checks/scale352.mjs.
  2. 2. The outside pictures with no door shape: give the witch's hut, the tents and the cave a door shape to judge, so an adult crawls in as they crawl out.
  3. 3. The three reference friends as a 🔬 preset: default, adult and child a tap apart, for checking a new place or door by eye.
  4. 4. A stoop: if the adult crawls too often to feel right, a ducking walk for 0.97 to 1.15 of a door.
  5. 5. Every new door sized to the default: a line in the agent instructions (written), and scale352 extended to each new door.

How this was put together

Written on 2026-10-18 by Claude, for Matthew, from ticket 352: Matthew, ticket ^ticket(352): "we crawl when we run out of tree houses, but on the exterior view, we are tall enough to go into the doors -- and quite small --- Fix this misalignment, and draft a report about the larger issue -- getting scale to be aligned; a special challenge for user-controlled characters." Then: "we need to bring background params and characters into alignment, focusing first on the default, and version that's 1.5 bigger, like an adult, and 0.5 size, like a small child." Every share was measured in the game in the app's browser: for each door with a shape, the friend's crown height at the door's depth (the rule doorTooLow uses [3]) over the door's opening, in every place, castle room and tree room, with the default body; the adult's and child's shares follow from the size slider, which scales the friend straight. The tree house's pairs were measured with each body and are kept by a check [2]; the fix is in 0.0.302 [1]. The data is in data/report.json; the page is built by drivers/build.py.
