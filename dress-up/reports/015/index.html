Seen before it was built

On 2026-10-20 Matthew asked for a dragon costume, fire to breathe and armour for the knight's horse. What did the two mock-ups show him before anything was built, what went out in 0.0.392, 81 minutes after he asked, and where does the game differ from them? From tickets ^ticket(499) and ^ticket(498): Matthew looked at both mock-ups on 2026-10-21, said “looks good” of each, and asked for them to be published as a report. Here they are, to play with, beside what went out in 0.0.392 (commit b1900d5e22, released 2026-10-20 19:14).

Short answers.

  1. Two mock-ups, ready 10 and 31 minutes after he asked. At 17:53 Matthew asked for a dragon costume (ticket ^ticket(499)) and for horse pieces for the knight (ticket ^ticket(498)), from Lego castles and knights. By 18:03 a page showed the friend in a dragon suit breathing six kinds of fire; by 18:24 a page showed the game's horse in six sets of armour. Each starts from the game's own drawing (the friend in the wolf suit; the horse side on), so what he saw was, side on, what the game would draw.
  2. He did not pick: all of it went out, for M and N to choose. At 18:48: “chickens dragon and horse -- just release and let the kids decide please.” 0.0.392, released at 19:14, 81 minutes after the first ask and 26 after his answer, has the costume, all six fires and all six armour sets, each a choice in the game.
  3. The mock-ups' drawings became the game's. Side on, the game's fires are the mock-up's shapes with the same timings, and their heat is the same six cones, 500 to 660 long (the friend standing is about 460) and 6 to 16 degrees each side, popping the bubbles it reaches, as Matthew asked at 17:58. The game then draws each piece again from the other sides, where each mock-up drew one: each fire toward us and away, the armour from the front and behind, led and ridden.
  4. Two proposals are not built yet: fire from things in a scene (a torch, a campfire, a cannon, a placed dragon), and armour pieces placed on their own (a chanfron on a stand). Smaller differences: a tap gives no puff of its own, and the plume swings with the horse's stride instead of swaying all the time.
  5. Checked, not yet timed. The three tickets' checks (armour498, dragon499, fire499) passed all 102 of their lines (59 + 18 + 25) in 0.0.392's run on GitHub and again in 0.0.397's. No frame-rate case yet breathes fire or rides the armoured horse.

The dragon and its fires: the mock-up

For ^ticket(499), ready at 18:03. The page draws the friend as the game draws them in the wolf suit, standing and facing right, recoloured, with the dragon's pieces added: horns, spines down the hood, teeth along the hood's opening, a slit eye on the hood, scales, a plated belly, claws, bat wings that flap, and a long tail with its spines standing up and a spade at its end. Pick a fire beside the picture or a card under it; hold “🔥 hold to breathe fire” (or F, once the frame has been clicked); untick “keep breathing” to see it stop. Bubbles drift in from the right, and the heat pops them. At the bottom, the page's own proposal: how it could go into the game.

Open the dragon mock-up on a page of its own: mockups/dragon-fire.html, 39,835 bytes, the file Matthew looked at, unchanged. Everything on it runs in the browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Heat that reaches past the flames

Matthew at 17:58: “let's have the dragon fire be the best tool against bubbles -- can you add logic for that -- the heat extend further than the fire.” In the mock-up each fire has a heat: a cone from the mouth, longer than its flames, shown as a dashed, shimmering edge (Figure 3). While the fire is on, any bubble whose edge is inside the cone pops, and the page counts them. The blue-hot jet reaches farthest and narrowest, the fire bubbles least far and widest; every one reaches past the friend's own height.

What went out in 0.0.392

After Matthew's answer at 18:48, the four things he released (the armour, the dragon, the fire and the stable's hens of ^ticket(506)) were built in parallel, each reviewed and fixed, then merged and released together as 0.0.392 at 19:14 (b1900d5e22) [3]. Its entry in the game's list of versions [4]: “Everything in 0.0.391, plus a dragon, fire, armour for the horse and a stable full of hens: every choice in the game, for the kids to pick.” Nothing of the dragon, the fire or the armour has changed since, to 0.0.397.

The dragon costume

🐯 Costume has a 🐉 dragon after the deer, with the mock-up's pieces and its four colours to start with, each with 6 to 8 swatches and “any color”. It is drawn facing us, from behind and from the side; standing, sitting, lying, crawling and swimming. Its wings flap with their own slow beat or with an LFO from the mixing board, stay open, or fold.

Breathing fire

🔥 Breathing fire is held, as 🫧 blowing bubbles is: 🔥 on the game controller (the right trigger), on the screen's controller, or F. Anyone can, whatever they wear. 🎮 Controls has a group, “🔥 breathing fire”: its fire (the six), from where (the 👄 mouth, the 🫲 left hand or the 🫱 right hand), how big (50 to 200) and whether its heat shows. The fire is drawn in a picture of its own laid over the scene, built once and then only moved; every shape in it is moved by a CSS animation, so the page does no work a frame for the flames. While it is held, the game works out each frame which bubbles are in its heat, and pops them: bubble space's and the smaller ones they throw, the bubble room's (at most 10 a frame), the bubble makers', the friend's blown ones and a diving suit's leaks. Let go, it fades in 0.2 s and nothing runs for it.

Side on, the six fires are the mock-up's: the same shapes, moved by the same keyframes. Toward us and away, each is a drawing of its own, not the side-on fire turned (Matthew's rule for a new angle): toward us it comes out of an open “O” mouth at us, its heat a disc; away it rises past the head.

The horse's armour

🧍 Body's 🐴 the horse has “🛡️ its armour”: none (to start with) or one of the six, under the mock-up's names. Each set is drawn side on, from the front and from behind, each a drawing of its own, led and ridden. The cloth and the plume take the knight's arms while the friend is a knight (the middle column below, in the mock-up's arms); otherwise the halter's colour and a lighter shade of it, quartered, with no emblem (the right column, where the friend is not a knight). The plumes and the parade's ribbons swing with the stride, as the tail does, and hang still while the horse stands.

Each was looked at as a still on the laptop (librsvg, through ffmpeg, which draws no animation: in such a still the mock-ups' flames, which take their colours from their animations, are black blobs at the mouth); in a browser they move. Pictures shown with <img> keep the light colours they were drawn in, on either theme.

The times are from the tickets' logs and the release's commits [3]; the check counts are the ok and FAIL lines in the two runs' logs; what shipped, and how, is read from the code at the tag [5] and the list of versions [4].
