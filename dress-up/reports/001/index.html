60 frames a second, and what checks it

Does Messed Up Dress Up hold 60 frames a second for what a game controller does in a place? How much of that do our tests check, and where do they run? From Matthew's rule, made on 2026-10-16 after the castle and 💭 our imagination dropped frames: "The priority is 60 FPS at all times, for all actions that a game controller does in the scene."

Short answers. Measured with the frame-rate gate (below) at 1920×1080 on Matthew's laptop, in headless Chrome; each figure the median of three runs.

  1. Where we measure, yes: every case holds 59.3 to 60 frames a second on 0.0.78 (fce3163): walking and dragging the friend in seven places. On 0.0.75, the same screen, 4 of the 10 cases in the first five places fell below 55, the lowest the castle drag at 45.8; and 💭 our imagination managed 18 on 0.0.77 (results, what it took).
  2. What we measure is narrow: 2 of the 8 kinds of controller action, in 7 of the 24 places (the castle's rooms and the tree house's count as one each). Running, crouching and crawling, the jumps, climbing, going through doors and into pictures, and the on-screen controller are not measured yet (coverage).
  3. None of it runs in CI. The repository has no workflows; GitHub only deploys the site. The gate runs by hand on Matthew's laptop before each release. A frame rate on a shared runner would mean little, but a count of what costs, per place, would not depend on the machine, and could fail a build (CI).
  4. What costs is kept off by default. Two things a background can draw, Gaussian blurs and the imagination's 22 pictures drawn live, are now settings in the game's ⚙️ Advanced tab, off until they can be made fast; on, walking in the imagination drops from 60 to 33 frames a second (settings). The slow computer the kids use, the black laptop, is not measured yet (the slow computer).

Other checks, not about frame rates, also run before a release: the sweep, which draws every place, castle room and costume and compares each drawing's hash with the last release's, so a change can't alter a drawing unnoticed; a dump of the settings and their rules; and checks of single features (the on-screen controller with several fingers at once, climbing and the picture that glows). What runs in CI

Nothing yet. The repository has no workflows: the one thing GitHub runs is its own deployment of the site, on every push. The gate and every other check run by hand on Matthew's laptop before each release, and their tools live outside the repository.

A frame rate measured in CI would not say much: GitHub's runners are shared and have no graphics card, and their speed varies from run to run, so a laptop's 60 is not a runner's. What could run in CI and mean the same everywhere is a count of what costs. Each kind of cost above is a countable thing in the drawing: masks on the friend, blurs, shapes drawn live in pictures, animations of a CSS property. A budget per place, counted at its defaults, would fail a build the moment a new mask or blur arrived on a path the friend moves through, before anyone felt it. Beside it, the drawings' checks can fail a build too, being exact, and the frame-rate gate can run on a runner as information, kept but not failing; the gate that fails stays the laptop's, before release.

The settings kept off until they are fast

In the game's ⚙️ Advanced tab since 0.0.78, each off unless switched on, on that computer only. Switched on, each says how many it draws in the place; 📏 measure here walks the friend for 2 seconds with it off and 2 with it on, and says what each managed. Matthew: "Each time we find some feature of the background that's threatening our FPS being 60 at all times, let's make it optional, and off by default, and add to the advanced panel in a similar way."

The slow computer

The kids also play on the black laptop, N's old one, where 💭 our imagination was too slow to walk in on 0.0.76, while walking with no background was fine. The gate runs on Matthew's much faster laptop and does not stand in for it yet. Chrome can slow a page's main thread (throttling) and can be started with a single thread for painting, which together come closer to an old computer; the gate has a slow mode for that, not yet set to match the black laptop. The surest measure is the black laptop itself: the ⚙️ Advanced tab's meter and 📏 run there with nothing to install.

What next

  1. 1. Add running, the two jumps and climbing a ladder by hand to the gate, in the places it already covers (climbing in 💭 our imagination, where the ladders are). Running changes more of the picture each frame than walking; a jump lifts the friend through more of it.
  2. 2. Measure on the black laptop itself, with the ⚙️ Advanced tab's meter and 📏 measure here in each covered place, then set the gate's slow mode (Chrome's CPU throttling and one raster thread) until it gives the same numbers, so the gate can stand in for it.
  3. 3. Cost budgets that CI can run: for each place at its defaults, count the masks, blurs, SMIL animations of CSS properties, and shapes drawn live, and fail when one goes over its budget (no masks on the friend, no blurs by default). They do not depend on the machine, so they can fail a build.
  4. 4. Move the test tools into the repository and add a workflow: the budgets and the drawings' checks (the sweep, the structure dump) failing the build, the frame-rate gate recorded alongside, not failing.
  5. 5. Cover touch: the on-screen controller's arrows and buttons, held and tapped, in the covered places (a phone's or tablet's screen size).

How this was put together

The gate is fpsgate.mjs (a copy in 001/drivers/), run against the game's own file in headless Chromium 155 started muted, at 1920×1080, on Matthew's laptop, with nothing else heavy running. For each place it loads the page once, sets the place from the game's defaults with the music muted, and runs a warm-up walk and drag, then three of each:

  • walk: a fake standard game controller (a Gamepad-shaped object the game reads as it reads a real one) holds ▶ for 1.5 s, then ◀ for 1.5 s;
  • drag: mouse events sent through the DevTools protocol press on the middle of the friend's body (facing us, so the press is not on an arm) and move right and back over 3 s, a move every 8 ms.

Frames are counted with requestAnimationFrame while the friend moves; a run's frames a second is the count over the time; a case's figure is the median of three runs, and it passes at 55 or more. The game's draw() is timed around each call. Causes were found with the gate's TWEAK (code run in the page before the runs, to freeze, hide or remove one thing at a time) and with dragtrace.mjs, which sums a Chrome performance trace of one walk or drag by thread and event, with Chrome's reasons for each invalidation. One run per variant was used to find causes; the figures in this report are the median-of-three gate runs.

The data is here twice: embedded in this page as JSON (<script type="application/json" id="report-data"> at its end: every gate run's raw readings, the versions and commits, and what each measure is), and as the gate's own files in 001/data/. The 0.0.77 run of the empty room and the imagination wrote no file; its numbers are copied from the gate's printed output, and its file says so. The page is made by 001/drivers/make.py from those files.
